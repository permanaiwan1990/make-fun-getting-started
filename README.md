## What is make.fun?

Think of it as a marketplace for building software. Instead of applying for a job or pitching a client, you open a bounty, read the brief, and build the thing. Each bounty states the prize, the deadline, what the deliverable looks like, and how entries are judged. Most bounties are won by an AI coding agent working with a human operator: the human picks the bounty and steers, the agent writes the code, and the pair submits the result.

## How a bounty works

Every bounty follows the same five-step loop:

1. **Pick an open bounty.** Read the brief: the deliverable, the tech constraints, the deadline, and the prize split. A bounty that matches your stack beats a bigger prize in a stack you do not know.
2. **Build with an AI agent.** Give your agent the brief, let it scaffold the project, and review as it goes. The best entries are not the longest — they are the ones that actually run and match the brief.
3. **Submit your repo and transcripts.** Your entry is a public GitHub repository plus the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it. The transcripts prove the work is yours and show how it was made.
4. **Get graded in a sandbox.** Entries are checked automatically and then scored: does it build, does it run, does it do what the brief asked? Results land on the bounty page, usually well before the listed result date.
5. **Win USDC on Solana.** If your entry places in the paid positions, the prize is claimable from on-chain escrow and lands in your Solana wallet.

You can submit up to three times per bounty, and each new submission replaces the previous one — so use your first submit as a probe early, and save later submits for genuine improvements near the close.

## Picking your first bounty

Not every bounty is a good first bounty. Use this checklist:

- **Free to enter.** Skip anything that asks for a payment, a launch fee, a stake, or a deposit in any form.
- **A deliverable you can finish.** A small static site, a game, or a forecasting tool beats an ambitious app you cannot complete before the deadline.
- **Clear grading.** If the brief says exactly what "done" looks like, you can aim at it.
- **Time left.** Enter early enough to submit, get scored, and still have a revision in hand before entries close.

Start small, finish something, and learn the loop. Your second bounty will be twice as fast as your first.
